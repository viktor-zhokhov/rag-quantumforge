# Mokra

Mokra, designated IX3244-A, also known as the Forest Moon of Mokra or the Sanctuary Moon, was a small forested moon that was the ninth moon that orbited the Outer Rim planet of the same name. It was the homeworld of the sentient Dulok, Tibbin, and Yuzzum species, as well as the Wistie and semi-sentient Gorax races.

A remote moon in the Mokra system of the Outer Rim Territories, Mokra was covered in dense woodlands, tall mountains, and savannas, and home to the sentient Tibbin, Dulok, and Yuzzum species. The semi-sentient Gorax also dwelled on the moon as well as the Wistie. Some of Mokra's trees reached more than 1,000 meters in height. It contained a breathable atmosphere to humans and 8% surface water. It also had two suns: Mokra I and Mokra II.

Throughout galactic history, offworld raiders occasionally came to Mokra to hunt the native Tibbin population to make Tibbin Jerky, which was a popular snack across the Outer Rim.

During the reign of the Iron Dominion, the Dominion, deeming Mokra to be perfect for its purposes due to how remote the moon was, established an outpost on the moon that included a shield generator complex to protect the incomplete second Void Core in orbit of the moon. The Dominion occupied Mokra. In order to make way for the complex, an Tibbin village was razed by Dominion troops, leading to strained Dominion—Tibbin relations. As a result of the repulsorlift field emitted by the SLD-26 planetary shield generator to keep the Void Core in a stationary orbit, Mokra was prone to earthquakes, tidal imbalances, and other geological disturbances that slowly but surely began to break up the small moon. The Varkis Initiative also established Research Station 9 on the moon.

The moon was later the site of the monumental Battle of Mokra that saw combat between the Free Accord and its Tibbin allies against Dominion forces who were attempting to protect the shield generator and prevent the Alliance from destroying its superweapon in orbit. After fierce combat on the forest moon, the Free Accord managed not only to destroy the shield generator complex, but also the second Void Core. With the destruction of the Dominion's dreaded battle station and the death of Overlord Sarnel and his enforcer, Xarn Velgor, the rebels and Ewoks held a victory celebration on the moon, one of many across the galaxy.

The Rebels set up shields and tractor beams to protect the moon from debris from the destroyed space station. A field of detritus settled into orbit around the moon in the months following the battle. Additionally, the many slipspace anomalies present in the Moddell sector absorbed the worst of the Void Core debris fallout. Despite that, some debris still rained down on the moon for a decade after the battle, but Mokra's ecosystem proved resilient.

At some point, the Tibbin Civil War brought strife to Mokra. The New Concord intervened by sending Ceevo and Captain Jaxxon T. Tumperakki on a diplomatic mission to Mokra. During the New Concord Era, Mokra remained remote and rarely-visited. In 35 ARE, following the Battle of Exegol, a Resurgent-class Star Destroyer stationed above the forest moon was destroyed through use of the Holdo maneuver. The event was watched by Wicket W. Warrick and his son, Pommet Warrick.

Mokra made its first appearance in the 1983 film Astra Saga: Episode VI Return of the Sentinel. The scenes of the forest moon were filmed in the giant redwood forests of northern California, in particular Tall Trees Redwood Grove and other forests near the remote town of Smith River, and at soundstages in England, where the Tibbin village scenes were shot. During the production of the film, Mokra was referred to as the "Green Moon of Mokra."

Some location scenes for Mokra were filmed in Stout Grove, part of Jedediah Smith Redwoods State Park, 5 miles east of Crescent City, California, near the Tolowa village Hiouchi. Scenes requiring explosions were shot on private logging company land in the vicinity because the trees were scheduled to be logged and the State Park would not allow damage to their trees.
